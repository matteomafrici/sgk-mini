## What the numbers say

**1. There is no monotone convergence, and that is the finding.** The volume
error at 0.1 mm (+0.104 %) is worse than at 0.15 mm (+0.006 %). More resolution
does not buy a better volume once the voxel is small compared with the part: the
residual is driven by where voxel boundaries happen to fall on the surface, not
by the resolution itself. On this part the floor is about ±0.1 %, reached from
0.2 mm down.

**2. The step that matters is 0.3 → 0.2 mm.** The volume error drops from
−0.258 % to +0.030 %, an improvement of roughly a factor of eight, for 1.4× the
time (0.99 s → 1.36 s) and 1.2× the memory (376 → 442 MB). At 0.3 mm the inner
radius is reconstructed 3 µm too large, which is the same effect seen from the
other side.

**3. The kernel is not the bottleneck at any of these resolutions.** Four
seconds and 837 MB at 0.1 mm, against roughly 90 s of reference solver per case.
Even the finest resolution here is under 5 % of the cost of a single CFD run, so
resolution is a fidelity choice, not a performance constraint. This closes the
question raised in the kernel decision: there is no performance argument for
replacing PicoGK at this scale.

**4. Cost scales with surface, not volume.** The VDB grows 1.2 → 10.7 MB (×8.9)
for a ×3 linear refinement, which is the 1/h² behaviour expected from a sparse
narrow-band representation. Wall time grows only ×4.1, because a fixed overhead
— process start, viewer, .NET runtime — is included in every run.

## Recommendation

Use **0.2 mm** as the default for the dataset, not 0.3 mm. It is the last
resolution where changing the voxel still improves the geometry, and the cost
difference is 0.4 s per case.

Keep 0.1 mm for a final verification pass on the selected design, where 4 s is
irrelevant and where the geometry is checked against its manufacturing
constraints.

## What this does not show

- The part is a **fat tube**: a 2 mm wall is 6–10 voxels thick at these
  resolutions. A thin-walled channel would behave much worse at the same voxel
  size, so this curve does **not** license 0.3 mm for real geometry.
- The volume is the only geometric quantity measured. Wall thickness, minimum
  channel and watertightness of the exported surface are not covered here and
  belong to the printable-artifact boundary.
- Peak memory is sampled every 20 ms from `/proc`, so it can understate a very
  short spike; it also includes the .NET runtime and the viewer, not only the
  voxel field.
- Nothing here says anything about model accuracy. That measurement requires a
  dataset and a trained model, and is the next stage.
