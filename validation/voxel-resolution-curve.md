## What the numbers say

**1. The volume error is not monotone, and that is the finding.** The error at
0.1 mm (+0.104 %) is larger than at 0.15 mm (+0.006 %). More resolution does not
buy a monotonically better volume once the voxel is small compared with the part:
the residual depends on where the voxel boundaries fall on the surface, not only
on the resolution. These four points do not establish a universal accuracy floor,
nor its cause; they establish that on this part the error is of order 0.1 % from
0.2 mm down.

**2. The step that matters is 0.3 → 0.2 mm.** The volume error drops from
−0.258 % to +0.030 %, an improvement of roughly a factor of eight, for about
1.5× the time and 1.2× the memory. At 0.3 mm the inner radius is reconstructed
3 µm too large. That is a real geometric error at that resolution; it is not
offered as the explanation of any CFD number.

**3. The kernel is not the bottleneck at any of these resolutions.** About four
seconds and 0.8 GB at 0.1 mm, against roughly 100 s of reference solver per
case. Even the finest resolution here is a few percent of the cost of a single
CFD run, so resolution is a fidelity choice, not a performance constraint. This
closes the question raised in the kernel decision: there is no performance
argument for replacing PicoGK at this scale.

**4. Cost scales with surface, not volume.** The VDB grows 1.2 → 10.7 MB (×8.9)
for a ×3 linear refinement, which is the 1/h² behaviour expected from a sparse
narrow-band representation. Wall time grows by about ×5, because a fixed
overhead — process start, viewer, .NET runtime — is included in every run.

## Recommendation

**Applied on 2026-11-18:** the default voxel size is now **0.2 mm**. It is the
last resolution where changing the voxel still improves the geometry, and the
cost difference is 0.4 s per case.

Keep 0.1 mm for a final verification pass on the selected design, where 4 s is
irrelevant and where the geometry is checked against its manufacturing
constraints.

## What this does not show

- The part is a **fat tube**: a 2 mm wall spans about 6.7 voxels at 0.3 mm and
  20 voxels at 0.1 mm. A thin-walled channel would behave much worse at the same
  voxel size, so this curve does **not** license 0.3 mm for real geometry.
- The volume is the only geometric quantity measured. Wall thickness, minimum
  channel and watertightness of the exported surface are not covered here and
  belong to the printable-artifact boundary.
- Peak memory is sampled every 20 ms from `/proc`, so it can understate a very
  short spike; it also includes the .NET runtime and the viewer, not only the
  voxel field.
- Nothing here says anything about model accuracy. That measurement requires a
  dataset and a trained model, and is the next stage.
